# 1. Purpose and scope

This protocol states, at the level of decisions rather than code, how the revised analysis answers the two questions of the paper and each reviewer concern. It was written after the first clean run (16 October) and before the final run; version 1.1 adds the analyses prompted by the verbatim reviewer text (19 October); its results are in `03_change_log.md` items 40–48. Version 1.2 (2 November) adds a post hoc minimum sample-size check (Riley et al. 2020; `tools/riley_samplesize.py`), RECORD/STROBE as complementary reporting guidelines for the registry component, and a work plan, timeline, risk register and quality-control checklist (`06_prompt_series_assessment`, §5); no pre-specified analysis was changed. Version 1.3 (2 November, after receipt of the registry data dictionary) adds a dictionary audit (stage 1e) and two sensitivity analyses for a stroke-anchored month-1 window (`death1` = No treated as unresolved when the onset-to-arrival delay exceeds 72 h, or is unknown); evidence grades are applied by the unchanged pre-specified rule. The implementation is `strokerev/` (one command, `python run_all.py --clean`); every number in the manuscript is generated by that run.

**Question 1 (ascertainment).** How was first-month vital status recorded in the registry over 2010–2023, which admissions have a determinable outcome, and what can be said about mortality in the whole registry?

**Question 2 (prediction).** Among admissions with a determinable outcome, how well do admission-time data discriminate first-month death, and which statements about calibration, risk tiers and clinical utility survive the selective ascertainment found under Question 1?

The questions are analysed and reported separately (reviewer A-2).

# 2. Design principles

| Principle | Consequence in the analysis |
|---|---|
| Evidence before inference | Raw workbook is read-only; every analytic record maps to its source rows; raw values are never overwritten; corrections live in separate columns and a log. |
| Nothing is forced | An outcome is assigned only when the evidence hierarchy supports it; everything else is *unresolved* and counted. |
| Assumptions are named | Two assumptions (A1, A2) that the registry documentation does not yet confirm are stated, and each label carries an evidence grade (E1/E0, R, I, U, X). |
| Selection is a parameter, not a nuisance | Unresolved outcomes are analysed as a selection process; registry-wide mortality is reported as bounds and assumption-dependent estimates, never as a single "true" rate. |
| One design, fixed first | Validation design, cut-off, cut-points and comparator were fixed before any model was evaluated; nothing is chosen on test data. |
| Reproduced ≠ re-analysed | Submitted counts that could be re-derived are labelled *reproduced*; model results that could not (code unavailable) are labelled *superseded by a new re-analysis*. |

```

  → endpoint assignment with provenance and grade                               [gate G1]
      labelled (R + I grades) ─┬─→ prediction analyses (Question 2)
      unresolved (U, X)        └─→ ascertainment analyses, all eligible (Question 1)
  → tables, figures, macros → manuscript → response letter (locations from compiled .aux)
  → verification (70+ checks) → readiness decision                              [gate G4]
```

Gate G1 (endpoint) is **open**: it closes only when the registry team answers questions Q1, Q2, Q4 and Q12 of the clarification request. Gate G2 is closed by rule-based decisions (source adjudication would upgrade it). Gate G3 (model validity) is closed by the new pipeline. Gate G4 is therefore *not ready* until G1 closes.

# 4. Endpoint (Question 1, reviewer R1-1, A-4)

- **Estimand.** Death from any cause within 30 days of admission (day 0 = admission date), including in-hospital death. No date of death exists in the workbook; the registry has been asked for the dates of the 33 post-discharge deaths (Q12).
- **Evidence hierarchy.** Contradictions first → unresolved. Then: in-hospital death with elapsed stay ≤ 30 d (event, grade I); in-hospital death > 30 d (non-event, I); `death1` = Yes after discharge (event under assumption A2, grade R); `death1` = No (non-event, R); discharged alive > 30 d (non-event, I); later contact alive when month-1 missing (non-event, R). Everything else unresolved (U) or undocumented code (X).
- **Why 30 days for in-hospital deaths.** Because the estimand is 30-day mortality from admission: an in-hospital death on day 31 is not a 30-day event. The stay distribution (median 4 d for in-hospital deaths; one death and 11 discharges in days 28–32) shows the boundary affects few labels.
- **Stay.** Date-derived when both dates are valid and the derived stay is 0–120 d; recorded length of stay otherwise; conflicts across day 30 without a month-1 status → unresolved.
- **Later contacts.** Used only when month-1 status is missing; they imply survival to day 30 under any time origin; their completeness by period is reported (87% of 2010–12 admissions, ≤ 16% from 2017) and the 20 labels they supply are removed in a sensitivity analysis.
- **Variants.** *Strict* (hospital-timed events only; A2 not used); *observed-only* (`death1` alone); submitted reconstruction re-implemented for audit only.
- **Tests.** 18 unit tests on boundary cases (day 30/31, discharge on day 30, later follow-up without date, contradictions, codes 9/3).

# 5. Ascertainment and registry-wide mortality (reviewer R1-2, R1-3, A-1)

1. **Describe recording over time**: completeness of `death1`, disposition, later contacts and follow-up-duration coding by admission year and period.
2. **Compare labelled and unresolved** patients on admission variables, missingness, period and disposition (standardised mean differences).
3. **Report registry-wide 30-day mortality as**: (a) logical bounds; (b) in-hospital 30-day mortality among patients with a known disposition; (c) inverse-probability-of-observation weighted estimate with positivity restriction (recorded disposition), weight truncation at p99, effective sample size, bootstrap that re-estimates weights; (d) pattern-mixture delta-adjustment over δ = 0.25–8 with two reference risks. No single value is called the true rate.
4. **Time-to-event methods are not used**: event and censoring times are not recorded (R1-3).

# 6. Prediction (Question 2, reviewer A-5, A-6, R1-4, R1-5, A-7, A-8)

| Element | Decision |
|---|---|
| Prediction time | Admission; every post-admission field excluded (stay, release date, disposition, follow-up). |
| Predictors | 27 admission variables with ≤ 60% missing; no engineered features; no data-driven selection. |
| Missing predictors | Chained-equation imputation inside every training fold (Bayesian ridge, ≤ 15 iterations; all predictors as auxiliary variables, outcome excluded); single posterior-mean imputation in the primary analysis; m = 5 posterior draws, missingness indicators for all predictors > 20% missing, and a low-missing-predictor model as sensitivity analyses. Mechanism discussed as MAR working assumption, MNAR not excluded. |
| Primary model | L2 logistic regression, no class weighting, penalty tuned in the inner loop. |
| Comparator | Pre-specified local clinical-reference logistic regression (age, NIHSS, GCS, admission mRS, haemorrhagic subtype, sex), same imputation, no tuning; not iScore/PLAN. |
| Challengers | Random forest, histogram gradient boosting, XGBoost, tuned in the inner loop. |
| Internal validation | Nested CV: 5 outer folds × 5 repeats, 3 inner folds; pooled out-of-fold AUROC with DeLong CI; fold-level mean ± SD. |
| Temporal validation | One cut-off fixed before modelling (70th percentile of admission dates among all eligible patients), applied identically to primary, strict and observed-only endpoints; case-mix, missingness and provenance shift described; period-membership AUROC reported; described as internal, not external. |
| Model comparison | Paired DeLong difference with 95% CI (nested pooled and temporal); fold-level differences; no equivalence claim without a margin (R1-4). |
| Calibration | Intercept, slope, Brier, grouped plots; primary model not recalibrated; Platt/isotonic fitted inside outer training folds as sensitivity only; IPW-weighted calibration (R1-5). |
| Risk tiers | Cut-points fixed before evaluation (< 10%, ≥ 40%); out-of-fold and temporal reported separately; patients per death captured reported as the downstream-effort illustration; no clinical-benefit claim (A-7). |
| Decision curves | 5–50% thresholds, unweighted and IPW; exploratory. |
| Interpretation | Bootstrap ORs and top-5 rank frequency; permutation importance on the temporal test set; SHAP for LR and XGBoost with rank concordance and feature-bootstrap interval; LIME removed; Glasgow-to-NIHSS ratio tested only as an added feature (A-8). |
| Subgroups | Sex, subtype, period, NIHSS recorded/missing, from out-of-fold predictions. |

# 7. Reporting and claims (reviewer A-3, A-13, A-9)

- Title, abstract, introduction and conclusions state the two questions separately and claim only what Sections 5–6 support.
- Withdrawn: "true" or "corrected" registry mortality; 14-point under-ascertainment; equivalence with the clinical model; temporal results as proof against dataset shift; "generalises across time"; stable explanations; priority ("first") claims; clinical benefit of tiers.
- Reproducibility reporting: software versions, seeds, hyperparameter grids, imputation settings, execution log, unit tests, verification report, TRIPOD+AI checklist with page locations, complete code supplied with the submission; the submitted analysis is reconciled item by item (Appendix B).
- Limitations name each weakness and the evidence that would resolve it (registry answers, source adjudication, external cohort).

# 8. Verification before any number is reported

1. Clean re-run from the raw workbook (`run_all.py --clean`), all stages logged.
2. Automated checks: checksum, row preservation, cohort arithmetic, leakage controls (predictor timing, fold-wise preprocessing, temporal cut-off independence, in-fold calibration), every hard-coded manuscript statement against outputs, LaTeX/BibTeX cleanliness, response-letter placeholders and page references.
3. Manual inspection of every figure and the compiled PDF.
4. Response-letter locations are read from the compiled manuscript, never typed.
